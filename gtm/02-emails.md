# Cold email — first touch

Two variants. Send half the list (5 firms) on Variant A, half on Variant B. Whichever pulls more replies wins for round 2.

**Rules of the road**:
- Send from Tommy's real Google Workspace inbox (not paid-app.com) for the first 10 — looks like a person, not a sequence
- One firm per email — no BCC, no mail-merge tells
- Subject ≤ 6 words, lowercase, no emoji, no "[Paid] —" prefixes
- Hit send between 7:30am and 9:00am local time for the recipient's city — first thing in their inbox
- If they don't reply in 4 business days, fire the LinkedIn DM (see 03-linkedin.md)
- Stop the sequence on any reply, even "not interested" — those replies are gold

## Variant A — pain-led

**Subject**: hours spent chasing invoices

> Hi {first_name},
>
> {Personal hook — one sentence drawn from their site, project, or LinkedIn. Example for Hunter Shadburne: "Saw your team is on the 2024 Austin civil rotation — those municipal Net-60s are brutal on cash flow."}
>
> Quick question: at {firm}, who's the person actually sending follow-ups when an invoice ages past 30 days — and how much of their week does it eat?
>
> I built Paid (paid-app.com) because I kept hearing principals at 10–25-person A/E firms say they were spending 4–8 hours a week on this themselves. It drafts polite reminders from your QuickBooks aging, you approve, it sends from your Gmail.
>
> Not pitching yet — I'm trying to talk to 10 boutique A/E firms about what actually breaks before I write another line of code. 25 minutes on Zoom this week or next?
>
> — Tommy

**Word count**: 121. Trim the hook if it doesn't fit. The question on line 3 is the actual ask — everything else is permission-to-ask scaffolding.

## Variant B — outcome-led

**Subject**: getting paid 12 days faster

> Hi {first_name},
>
> {Hook — same as Variant A}.
>
> The thing I've heard from 4 other boutique firms this month is that just sending an actual reminder on day 31 (instead of "let me get to it next week") moves average days-to-pay from 47 → 35. Pure cash-flow gain, no client gets upset.
>
> Paid (paid-app.com) does that part for you — drafts the reminder from QuickBooks, you read it in Gmail, you send. I'm trying to talk to 10 firms in the {discipline} space before I commit roadmap.
>
> Worth 25 minutes this week? I'll trade you a free trial + the templated reminder library either way.
>
> — Tommy

**Word count**: 113. The "12 days faster" claim needs to be true before you put it in writing — if you don't have data on this yet, swap for "get paid sooner without nagging your clients."

## Day-3 bump (if no reply)

**Subject**: re: hours spent chasing invoices (or re: getting paid 12 days faster)

> {first_name} — short bump in case the first one buried. Still curious who at {firm} owns the follow-up grind, and if 25 minutes is worth it. If not, just reply "no" and I'll stop bugging you.
>
> — Tommy

**Word count**: 35. The "reply 'no'" gives them permission to opt out — counterintuitively raises reply rates by ~30%.

## What "yes" looks like — instant reply template

> Awesome. Two options:
> - {Calendly link with 3 25-min slots this week}
> - Or reply with 3 windows in your tz and I'll send an invite
>
> No prep needed. I'll ask about your A/R workflow, you ask about Paid.

Get a Calendly free tier set up — paste the link here before sending the first email. Without it you'll lose half your "yes" replies to scheduling friction.

## Anti-patterns — do not do these

- ❌ "I noticed you're a leader in the architecture space" — too generic, screams template
- ❌ "Hope you're well" — instant signal it's a cold pitch
- ❌ Three paragraphs of company background — they don't care
- ❌ "Quick demo?" without context — demos are for after the discovery call
- ❌ "Let me know if interested" — no, give them a specific question to answer
